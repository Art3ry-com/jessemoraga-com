How I Build Software With AI — With No CS Degree

No engineering pedigree. One operator. A real services business with real customers — and software I built with AI to run all of it. Here's the actual system.

JM
Jesse Moraga
Jun 14, 2026 · operator & builder
[ hero image — drop cover ]

People assume "builds software with AI" means a chatbot bolted onto a landing page. It doesn't. It means the boring middle of a business — quoting, scheduling, follow-up, invoicing, reviews — runs on software I built with AI and an operator (me) who knows exactly where every decision happens. No CS degree. A surgical-tech background, a lot of reading, and a refusal to ship anything I can't explain.

This post is the real architecture: what's automated, what stays human, and the one principle that keeps it from collapsing.

"AI doesn't remove the operator. It moves the operator up a level — from doing the work to designing the system that does it."

One brain, one source of truth

Every workflow reads and writes from a single model of the business. A lead, a job, a customer — there's exactly one of each, and everything else points at it. The moment you have two "sources of truth," automation starts fighting itself.

The follow-up runs itself

The single highest-leverage automation isn't fancy. It's making sure no lead ever sits unanswered. Here's the core of the responder — event-driven, idempotent, and dead simple to reason about:

on_new_lead.js 
// fires the moment a lead lands — any channel
export async function onNewLead(event) {
  const lead = await upsertContact(event.payload);
  // idempotent: re-running never double-sends
  if (lead.contactedAt) return;

  await send({
    to: lead.phone,
    body: `Hey ${lead.firstName} — got your request. 
           Quick question to get you a quote…`,
  });
  await schedule("followup.nudge", { in: "2h", leadId: lead.id });
}

That if (lead.contactedAt) return line is the whole reliability story. Events fire more than once in the real world; idempotency means that's fine.

Where the hours actually went

When I logged a month of operations before and after, the shape of my week changed completely. The work didn't disappear — it moved off me and onto the system.

The part I keep human

Pricing judgment, the hard conversations, and anything a customer would feel as "automated and cold." The software clears the runway; I still fly the plane. That balance — not the tooling — is what makes it sustainable solo, while raising my daughter, without the whole thing running my life instead of the other way around.

If you take one thing: don't automate a process you don't understand. Map it by hand first. Then let the machine run the version you already trust.
